PURPOSE
Rate the builders and clients we work for, so tender selection is based on evidence rather than memory. Feeds the prequalification decision and the tender pipeline.


GRADING
Each criterion is scored 1 to 4 (1 Poor, 2 Fair, 3 Good, 4 Excellent) across 14 criteria. Scores are totalled out of 52.
  A (Excellent)  42–52   80%+    Highly valued. Prioritise work with them. Consistently meet or exceed expectations and align with our brand values and project styles.
  B (Good)       32–41   60–79%  Generally reliable. Keep building the relationship; take the work when the workload allows.
  C (Poor)       0–31    under 60%  Significant issues and risk. Avoid unless there is a strong reason, and proceed with extreme caution.

Provisional grades: a new client with no completed jobs starts at a provisional C; where there is some data but not enough, a provisional B. Review provisional grades monthly for the first three months.

When to review: at least every six months; after each significant project (with a team debrief); and after any significant event such as a major payment delay, contract dispute or safety violation.


THE 14 CRITERIA (4 points / 3 / 2 / 1)
1. Tendering & pricing — accepts fair pricing and values quality, signs and starts quickly / generally accepts pricing, minor delays / always chases lowest price, noticeable delays / demands unreasonable discounts, stalls the job.
2. Pre-project negotiation — professional, fair, efficient / reasonable with minor issues / difficult, significant compromises / unreasonable and adversarial.
3. Contract & variation terms — clear and mutually agreeable / mostly fair / unclear or one-sided, frequent disputes / unacceptable, constant disputes.
4. Scope of works & expectations — realistic and well defined / mostly realistic / unrealistic, frequent changes / completely unrealistic, constantly shifting.
5. Project programming — timely and collaborative / generally timely, minor delays / delays and little collaboration / significant delays, no collaboration.
6. Financial stability — established and strong / generally stable / newer or some instability / significant financial concerns.
7. Safety & compliance — proactive, adheres to all requirements / generally compliant / some issues, needs reminders / significant violations.
8. Technology & systems — efficient use of project tools (HammerTech, ITPs) / generally uses them / inconsistent, needs help / refuses to use required systems.
9. Communication — clear, prompt, respectful / generally good / inconsistent or unclear / poor, unresponsive or disrespectful.
10. On-site coordination — well organised, easy access / generally good / some access or coordination issues / poor, significant delays.
11. Payment history — always on time or early / mostly on time / frequent delays needing reminders / consistently late, disputes or non-payment.
12. Repeat business potential & brand alignment — strong fit with small commercial and custom architectural work, likely repeat and referrals / good fit / possible under conditions / unlikely, misaligned.
13. Overall profitability — consistently meets or exceeds margin targets / generally meets / sometimes falls below / consistently low or no profit.
14. Scope & expectations, programming and the remaining criteria are scored on the same 1–4 basis.

HOW THIS SHOULD BE USED
- Prequalification: the client's grade belongs in the prequalification decision alongside scope, materials, storeys and distance. An A client with a marginal job is a different decision from a C client with the same job.
- Pricing and risk: a C client's payment and variation history is a real cost. It belongs in the risk allowance rather than being absorbed as optimism.
- Job results: once the job results register is running, actual labour return and margin by client replaces judgement on criteria 13 (profitability) with evidence.
- Growth: the marketing strategy targets architects and premium builders. The A-graded builders here are the relationships worth deepening.
