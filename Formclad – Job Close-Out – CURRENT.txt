FORMCLAD – JOB CLOSE-OUT – CURRENT
Version 1.0 | 18 September 2026
Owner: Operations Approver (Director) for the site and documentation; Commercial Approver (General Manager) for the money.

WHY
The end of a job is where money is left behind: a final claim not made, a variation never priced, retention that nobody asks for, or a defect that reopens a finished job. It is also the only moment when what actually happened is still fresh enough to improve the next estimate. Close-out should start before practical completion, not after everyone has moved on.

Trigger: when the job is roughly two weeks from finishing on site. Not after.


1. BEFORE PRACTICAL COMPLETION
[ ] Walk the job with the builder's supervisor and agree the defect list in writing. A list you agreed beats a list you receive.
[ ] Finish outstanding work and rectify defects; photograph completed work before access equipment leaves site.
[ ] Confirm the final clean is done — no swarf or debris left on sheeting (it rusts, and it comes back as a defect months later).
[ ] Check every variation is identified, priced, submitted and approved. Anything unapproved goes to both approvers now, not at claim time.
[ ] Confirm all site instructions, directions and delay notices are recorded.
[ ] Book removal of access equipment, edge protection, waste and surplus material. Return unused stock and credit it to the job.

2. DOCUMENTATION FOR HANDOVER
[ ] ITP signed off (the Goodwood Rd roof plumbing ITP is the current reference).
[ ] Installation checks and site audits completed in the Nexvia app.
[ ] O&M manual compiled and issued. Use the O&M Manuals folder in AdminHub > Operations & Systems, and the product data in Material Tech Data.
[ ] Warranties issued: Formclad's 12-month workmanship guarantee plus material warranties from the suppliers.
[ ] As-installed information, product data sheets and maintenance requirements included.
[ ] Copy of everything issued saved in the project folder before it goes.
Note: the builder often will not certify the final claim or release retention until this pack is complete. Treat it as a payment step, not paperwork.

3. FINAL CLAIM AND MONEY
[ ] Final claim raised, including all approved variations.
[ ] Check for unbilled work: approved variations not yet claimed, uncertified claims, back charges disputed or accepted.
[ ] Confirm the claim method for this builder (Nexvia and email, PayApps, or RCTI).
[ ] Record the retention in the retention register in Nexvia: amount held, practical completion date, defects liability period end, and both release dates.
[ ] Diarise the retention release dates. Retention is money already earned; it only comes back if someone asks.
[ ] Confirm the defects liability period start and end in writing.

4. JOB RESULT (within two weeks, while it is fresh)
[ ] Quoted labour hours versus actual, and where the difference came from.
[ ] Quoted labour return per hour versus achieved.
[ ] Quoted gross margin versus achieved.
[ ] Material wastage used versus the allowance (simple sheets +5%, complex +10–15%, flashings +10%, fasteners +10%).
[ ] Whether the labour difficulty factors applied at tender matched site reality.
[ ] Two sentences from Travis: what we would price differently, and what we would build differently.
[ ] Add the row to the job results register.
[ ] Feed anything that recurs into the Nexvia estimate master and the QA checklist.

5. THE BUILDER
[ ] Review the client rating: programming, coordination, communication, payment history, contract and variation behaviour, profitability. Update the ratings tool and republish the grades file.
[ ] Note anything that should change how we price or qualify their next job.
[ ] If the job went well, ask about upcoming work while the relationship is warm.

6. FILING AND SYSTEMS
[ ] Project folder complete: contract, drawings, variations, claims, safety and quality records, handover pack, photographs.
[ ] Move the project folder to Archived Jobs.
[ ] Close the project in Nexvia once the final claim and retention arrangements are recorded, so it stops appearing in live job reporting.
[ ] Photographs worth using: add good ones to the marketing set. The capability statement needs project spotlights and gallery images, and finished jobs are where they come from.

7. DEFECTS LIABILITY PERIOD
[ ] Diarise the DLP end date.
[ ] Record any defect callback against the job so its true cost is visible.
[ ] Claim the final retention release when due.
