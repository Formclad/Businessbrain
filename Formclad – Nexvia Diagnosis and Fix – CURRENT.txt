FORMCLAD – NEXVIA: WHAT IS WRONG AND HOW TO FIX IT – CURRENT
Version 1.2 | 19 September 2026
Owner: Sofia (General Manager)

Built from: the Formclad Nexvia admin settings (read 19 September 2026), the
V7.3 estimate import master, the Nexvia public knowledge base, and the
Formclad-Nexvia correspondence and meeting records from February to September 2026.


THE SHORT VERSION

Nexvia is not misconfigured. It is half-configured, and the half that is
missing is the half only Formclad can supply.

Everything a vendor can do alone has been done: Xero is connected and live,
the chart of accounts is loaded, tax rates are set, a labour rate card exists.
Everything that required Formclad to decide how its own work breaks down has
not been done: cost categories, cost codes, services, item groups and material
cost categories are all empty.

There are two root causes, and between them they produce nearly every symptom.

  ONE - the cost structure was never built. Cost categories, cost codes and
  material cost categories are empty, so bills have nothing to land on. There
  are also no purchase orders, so bills have no PO line to match against
  either. Hence Miscellaneous.

  TWO - estimates are built flat, with no fixtures. Extent of Works is
  generated from top-level fixtures, so with none there is nothing to generate
  and labour has nowhere to sit. Hence Unassigned.

And the two are connected: where "Cost to Complete - Use Extents" is on, the
missing extents also empty the breakdown list that bills must be coded to. See
THE THREE MACHINES.

Everything else follows. The breakdown offers nothing but "default" because
nothing is tagged. Every project is structured differently because the V7.3
master contains two contradictory standards. The progress claim shows two
levels because the wrong import mode is selected. Profit per job cannot be
trusted because half the costs are unallocated and the labour rate exists in
three conflicting versions.

These are not eight problems. They are two, plus their consequences.

The fix is roughly two days: one day of decisions, one day of entry. It is not
ongoing work, and nothing else worth doing is possible until it happens -
including any AI automation of the administration.

THE THREE MACHINES

The single most useful thing to understand about Nexvia is that three
different systems look similar, are easily confused, and do not overlap at
all. Most of the confusion to date comes from using one to try to fix another.

  EXTENT OF WORKS - labour only.
  A list of work items with a name, a quantity and allocated hours. When crew
  log time they pick an extent and a labour type, and the hours land against
  it. That is all it does. It has no effect on bills, costs or what the client
  sees.

  BREAKDOWN - client presentation only.
  A set of custom headings. Estimate lines are pulled under those headings by
  Group ID. It sits on Commercial Tenders and is deliberately independent of
  the assembly tree. That independence is the point of it.

  BUDGET COST CODING - where bills land.
  Cost categories, cost codes and material cost categories. Separate again.

Extents do not affect claims. Breakdowns do not affect labour tracking.

BUT NOTE: "breakdown" means three different things in Nexvia, which is a large
part of why this has been so hard to reason about.

  1. The TENDER BREAKDOWN - client quote headings, populated by Group ID.
  2. The COST TO COMPLETE BREAKDOWN - what each cost is assigned to. This is
     the one being chosen when importing a bill.
  3. The EXTENT OF WORKS BREAKDOWN - budget versus actual per extent.

And the three machines are connected at one point. When "Cost to Complete -
Use Extents" is ticked on a project, the Cost to Complete breakdown options
BECOME the extents. Our Lady has that setting on and has no extents, so the
list is empty - which is exactly the reported symptom of "the breakdown
doesn't exist, it just says miscellaneous or default".

So the bills problem and the labour problem are not two independent failures.
No fixtures in the estimate means no extents on the project, which means the
crew's hours have nowhere to land AND bills have nothing to be coded against.
One cause, two symptoms, two different screens.

  TO CONFIRM: that the empty extent list is what empties the bill breakdown
  dropdown. It fits every symptom and every document, but worth eyeballing on
  a live bill before building a plan on it.


WHY LABOUR GOES TO UNASSIGNED

The crew can only book time against an extent that exists. If a project has no
Extent of Works, every hour falls into Unassigned and has to be placed by hand.

Our Lady Queen of Peace (J001217) shows it plainly: 53 hours allocated against
the Site labour group with zero used, and 157.5 hours sitting in Unassigned at
$11,303.88. Cost to Complete then reports "Not Allocated $11,303.88" and the
budget summary reads minus 22.65% margin. The job is not necessarily losing
money. The hours have nowhere to sit. (Some of those hours are test entries
and should be removed before that project's numbers are used for anything.)

Extents are created per project, not globally. The step is being skipped at
conversion - and when it has been tried, it produced something not worth
keeping. That is a real finding, not a failure of nerve, and it has a cause.

  ACTIVATION CONVERTS TOP-LEVEL FIXTURES INTO EXTENTS.

  Not assemblies. Fixtures. This single fact governs everything below, and it
  is confirmed in Nexvia's own documentation: "The Extent of Work is based on
  the top-level Fixtures in the Assemblies. In each Fixture (i.e. Extent of
  Work) is the labour that has been estimated to produce the Fixture."

  Fixture = Extent of Work. One to one.

  IMPORTANT: only the TOP level counts. The activation dialog "only lists the
  top-most level of Fixtures and does not list fixtures that are within other
  Fixtures". A nested fixture never becomes an extent. The standard therefore
  needs exactly ONE level of fixtures - the five work packages, no deeper.

  Also note: the default Allocated Percent is set under Settings > Subsidiary
  in the Admin Portal and can only be changed by Nexvia. Ask Tosin what
  Formclad's is set to before running this on a live job, since it decides how
  many of the quoted hours are allocated by default.

  Formclad estimates have been built flat - Assembly = work package, no
  fixtures - following the older standard in V7.3. With no fixtures to convert,
  there is nothing to split by and the hours collapse into a single lump per
  assembly. Our Lady's Budget > Hours holds exactly one row, "Roofing Labour -
  Site", 53 hours, for the whole job.

  So the choice on offer was a single meaningless bucket, or the "Create Group"
  / "Create All" option, which consolidates each labour type across the whole
  job into one extent and deletes the individual rows. Nexvia's own wording:
  it "allows a Labour Type to be removed from the individual Extents and added
  as an Extent". That produces extents called "Roofing Labour" and "Cladding
  Labour" - which tells you nothing, because labour type is already a separate
  field on the time entry.

  Neither option is worth having. The feature is not the problem. The estimate
  structure feeding it is.

  DO NOT USE "Create All" or "Create Group". Build fixtures in the estimate and
  let the fixtures become the extents.

  Fix, per job, about ten minutes - once estimates carry fixtures:
    1. Convert the tender to a project.
    2. Budget > Hours > "Import Labour Materials" to create the budgeted hours
       rows from the assembly labour.
    3. Check the rates on those rows (see THE LABOUR RATE PROBLEM below -
       do not skip this).
    4. Budget > Assembly > Options > "Activate Extent of Work". Leave the
       fixture rows as they are. Check the allocated percentages, adjust hours
       if needed, then "Create Extent of Works".
    5. Confirm on the project Modify screen that Mobile Time Tracking,
       Production Time Tracking, Enable Cost to Complete and "Cost to Complete
       - Use Extents" are ticked.
    6. Open the app on one job and confirm the crew see the work packages -
       Roof Covering, Roof Plumbing and so on - not a list of labour types.

  WARNING: "Create Extent of Works" can only be run ONCE per project. Later
  edits go through Admin portal > project > Extent of Works tab. The 'Extent of
  Works' permission must be set on the user card.

  TO CONFIRM: the exact behaviour when an assembly has no fixtures is inferred
  from the documentation and from Our Lady's single budget row. Worth verifying
  on a live project before relying on it.

  Build the extent grid once on the next job and save it as a template.
  Templates carry labour types, allocated hours, quoted hours and quantities,
  and import into any project. After that, setup is import, adjust hours, done.

  Existing jobs with unassigned hours - Xavier College, 8 Barrelder Ave,
  Colonist Hotel, Goodwood Rd and others - need the Admin portal route, since
  activation from the assembly is one-shot. Worth doing on active jobs so
  claims can be supported. Jobs near completion probably are not worth it.


WHY BILLS GO TO MISCELLANEOUS

Two causes stacked on top of each other, which is why it has felt unfixable.

  First: there is nothing to code a bill to. Cost Categories, Cost Codes and
  Material Cost Categories are all empty. Nexvia's own description of the last
  of these is "material cost categories for actual matching when using a
  breakdown" - the exact mechanism that is failing, and the table is blank.
  Where "Use Extents" is on, the list is the extents - which do not exist
  either.

  Second: there are no purchase orders to match against. Supplier bills are
  designed to match PO lines on receipt. Formclad does not raise POs, so there
  is no line to match and every bill needs manual coding - into a structure
  that does not exist.

The February plan was to introduce POs for supervisors. It was never done.

On the bulk import failures specifically: Nexvia's chart of accounts holds
twelve accounts, and all of them are job costs -

  200      Sales (Income)
  210      Service Income (Income)
  305.1    Consultant Fees - Project-specific
  310      Cost of Goods Sold
  311      Labour Hire
  312      Hire of Plant & Equipment
  315      Materials & Supplies
  352      Freight & Delivery - Job-specific
  475      Subcontractors
  477      Wages & Salaries - Direct
  480      Safety Costs - Dry
  860      Rounding

Xero holds the full chart, including every overhead. When the bulk import runs
across all Xero bills, anything coded to an overhead account hits an account
Nexvia has never had and fails. Account 454 is one of those.

  Do NOT fix this by adding 454 to Nexvia. That would pull overheads into job
  costing and destroy the profit-per-job figure this is all meant to produce.
  Fix it upstream: filter the Xero export so only job-coded bills come across,
  and add to Nexvia only those accounts that genuinely are job costs and are
  genuinely missing.

This has been running since at least 7 May, when a session was held titled
"Bill Imports & Xero Chart of Accounts".


WHY THE PROGRESS CLAIM SHOWS TWO LEVELS

This is an import mode, not a hierarchy problem.

When a progress claim is built, Accounts > Client Progress Claims > "Import
Budget Data" offers a dropdown of import modes:

  Project Breakdown             top-level headings only
  Project Breakdown (Expanded)  headings plus every line
  Project Budget                consolidated budget sections
  Assemblies                    each assembly, with fixtures and items as
                                separate claim lines

Formclad is using Assemblies. That mode is designed to break fixtures and
items out as individual claim lines - which is why Roof Plumbing arrives with
Custom Orb underneath it.

  Fix: build a Breakdown with the headings Formclad actually claims against -
  Roof Covering & Insulation, Roof Ancillaries, Roof Plumbing, Wall Cladding,
  Wall Ancillaries - and use Project Breakdown mode. One level, every job,
  whatever the estimate looks like underneath.

  Selecting Project Breakdown opens a SECOND dropdown, "Select a breakdown".
  Formclad's will read "Default" and be empty, because the tender breakdown was
  never built. That is the "it just says default" symptom. The preview also
  carries a line called "Unassigned Costs" - the catch-all for anything with no
  Group ID, which on current jobs would hold nearly everything.

THE RED ALERT ICON. If a red icon appears beside "Total Budget Ex" after
import, it means the figure does not match the current Running Sale price as at
the last budget check. Two fixes: run a Budget Check in the Budget Summary, or
adjust the budget figures directly. So a claim that will not reconcile is
usually reporting that the budget has not been checked since something moved -
not that the claim is broken.

AFTER IMPORT, use "Lock Totals" to stop the figures being edited underneath
you. The action is logged and reversible. It needs the Lock/Unlock Client
Progress Claims permission on the user card.

TRAP: changing the client on Summary > Modify WIPES the entire Client Progress
Claim section, because the section is client-specific. Budget data can be
re-imported afterwards, but any manual claim work is gone. Finish the claim
work before touching the client.

ACCESS: Client Progress Claims requires being the Project Manager for the
project, a member of the Accounts Management team, or being added manually via
the Access tab.

On the maths not reconciling: a Group ID assigned to a parent Assembly or
Fixture row is NOT inherited by the fixtures, materials or hours inside it.
Services and Items inherit. Assemblies do not. Any line not individually
tagged sits outside the breakdown and the totals will not add up.

Tosin said exactly this on 17 August - every item in the assembly needs
tagging, fixtures, materials and labour. It was filed as a note about a report.
It is the answer to the claim problem.


WHY EVERY PROJECT IS SET UP DIFFERENTLY

The V7.3 master contains two contradictory hierarchy standards in the same
workbook.

  The Notes sheet and the Copilot prompt say: Assembly = whole job, building
  or stage. Fixture = commercial work package (Roof Covering & Insulation,
  Roof Ancillaries, Roof Plumbing, Wall Cladding, Wall Ancillaries).

  The Import Standard sheet says: Assembly = commercial work package, items sit
  directly under Assemblies by default, Fixtures are optional and should not be
  created casually - and lists Roof Covering & Insulation as an Assembly.

These produce completely different trees. The sheet labelled legacy also calls
itself the current source of truth. The Copilot prompt references an "Import
Standard V7" sheet.

Whichever sheet is followed on the day determines the shape of the job, and
since the assembly tree feeds the breakdown, the extents and the claim, that
choice propagates into everything downstream.

  These two are NOT equally valid. The V7 structure is the correct one, and
  the reason is Extent of Works:

    Activation converts top-level FIXTURES into extents. Under the V7
    structure, the fixtures are Roof Covering & Insulation, Roof Ancillaries,
    Roof Plumbing, Wall Cladding and Wall Ancillaries - which become exactly
    the extent list the crew should be choosing from, and exactly the level
    Formclad claims at. Under the flat structure there are no fixtures, so
    there is nothing to convert and labour has nowhere to go.

  The flat standard also looks attractive because it seems to fix the
  two-level progress claim. It does not - that is an import mode, handled
  above - and it breaks labour tracking as the price of a problem it was never
  solving.

  Fix: make V7 authoritative. Assembly = the building or stage. Fixtures = the
  five standard work packages, at one level only. Delete the older Import
  Standard sheet from the master rather than leaving it in as "legacy",
  because it is currently being followed. Reissue as V8 with one standard in
  it and a line explaining why fixtures are mandatory.

  Resolve this before the next tender is priced, not after. Every estimate
  built flat between now and then is another project that cannot track labour
  and cannot code its bills.


THE LABOUR RATE PROBLEM

There are three rate cards for the same labour types, and they disagree.

  Roofing Labour, cost per hour / charge-out:
    V7.3 master (Labour_Factors)     $84.00 / $110.00
    Our Lady project budget row      $84.00 / $115.00
    Nexvia Labour Type settings      $80.00 / $120.00

The V7.3 Commercial Settings sheet records $84.00 as CURRENT,
management-confirmed 4 September 2026. Formclad Pricing Settings say $80.00.

This is why $84 becomes $80 on import. It is not a bug. V7.3's own
specification states that where Type = Labour, the unit cost is taken from the
matched Labour Type - so Nexvia substitutes its own figure and the
spreadsheet's is discarded. Marianne reported the symptom in July.

  Fix: decide the productive field labour cost once - $80 or $84 - and the
  charge-out for each labour type once. Then update, in this order:
    1. Nexvia Labour Type settings (these win on import)
    2. V7.3 Labour_Factors and Commercial Settings
    3. Formclad Pricing Settings in the Brain
    4. Any existing project budget rows still carrying old rates

  Until this is done, no profit-per-job or ROR figure from Nexvia can be
  trusted, because the cost side is wrong by up to 5%.

The full Nexvia labour type table as at 19 September 2026:

  Travel, Admin/Accounts, Contracts Admin      $60.00 cost / $90.00  charge
  Apprentice, Trade Assistant                  $70.40       / $110.00
  Cladding, Roofing, Site Labour - General     $80.00       / $120.00
  Inspections & Reports, Set Out / Measure     $84.00       / $125.00
  Leading Hand / Supervisor, Away / Remote     $84.00       / $130.00
  Project Management                           $98.60       / $135.00
  Double Time - Labour                         $168.00      / $200.00

Note also: the Rates table (used for payroll export) is empty. Normal time,
time and a half and double time are all $0.00, with one active rate called
"Default" at zero and "Supervisor" deleted. This may be legitimate if it is
only used for casual staff, but it is worth confirming, since payroll export
was one of the February priorities.


WHAT IS EMPTY, AND WHAT IS NOT

  Empty:
    Cost Categories
    Material Cost Categories
    Cost Codes
    Services
    Item Groups
    Extent of Works (global template list)
    Rates (all values $0.00)

  Set up and working:
    Xero - connected to "Formclad 2026", active, payroll active, job number
      and name exported to reference and narration
    Chart of Accounts - 12 entries, all job costs
    Tax Rates - GST 10%, FRE 0%, GNR, N-T
    Ext of Work Types (Labour Types) - 14 types with cost and charge-out
    Labour Groups - Office, Site

  Available but not connected:
    Payapps - inactive, never connected (token expiry 01/01/1970). Note this
      integration syncs against purchase order lines, so it is built for the
      party awarding the contract. It will not push Formclad's claims up to a
      builder's Payapps. It could in principle be used for Formclad's own
      subcontractors.
    PayHero, Lentune Import, Mozaik Import - not assessed.

  Named misleadingly - three to know about:
    "Contract Management" does not manage contracts. It adds custom variable
      fields to purchase order templates. No head contracts, subcontracts,
      variations, retentions or claims.
    "Workflow" does not automate anything. It is a project checklist -
      sections (Award, Project, Handover), tickable items, critical flags,
      progress bars. It can be pushed to the Client Portal, which is a
      reasonable way to show a builder progress without writing an email, but
      it has no triggers, notifications or document generation.
    "Breakdown" means three different things - see THE THREE MACHINES above.


THE FIX, IN ORDER

Everything below depends on the thing above it. Doing them out of order wastes
the work.

  1. Settle the labour rate. One cost, one charge-out per type, updated in
     Nexvia first. Half a day, mostly decision.

  2. Resolve the V7.3 hierarchy contradiction. Make V7 authoritative -
     Assembly = building or stage, Fixtures = the five standard work packages
     at one level. Delete the older flat standard from the master. Reissue as
     V8. Half a day, and the single highest-value item on this list, because
     every estimate priced flat between now and then is another project that
     can neither track labour nor code its bills.

  3. Build the cost structure. Cost categories, cost codes and material cost
     categories that match how Formclad actually buys and builds. Then a
     services and item library so estimates are assembled from components
     rather than from scratch. One day of decisions with Travis, one of entry.

  4. Build the Breakdown headings and switch progress claims to Project
     Breakdown mode. Half a day. Fixes the two-level claim and the maths.

  5. Run the extent setup on the next won job, save it as a template, then
     back-fill active jobs through the Admin portal. Ten minutes per job after
     the first.

  6. Introduce purchase orders, starting with supervisors and a dollar
     threshold. This is what makes committed costs and Cost to Complete work.
     Ongoing change, not a one-off task.

  7. Fix the Xero bill export filter so only job-coded bills come across.

Only after 1-5 is the data good enough to automate anything against.


ON AUTOMATING THE ADMINISTRATION

Nexvia will not do this. Its Workflow module is a checklist, not an automation
engine, and there is nothing else in the product that generates or chases
administration. The automation has to come from outside - the Brain, reading
Nexvia's data - which is what was already being reached for.

The ambition is right and the order matters. Anything automated - by Claude,
by a Copilot agent, by a Power Automate flow - reads the same data a person
does. While labour is unassigned and bills are miscellaneous, automation
produces confident, well-formatted, wrong answers faster than anyone can check
them, and the errors are harder to spot because they look finished.

Once the structure above exists, the automation becomes straightforward,
because the questions have real answers to read: what did this job cost against
budget, which labour type is over, what is committed but not yet invoiced,
what should this month's claim be.

The structure is the prerequisite, not a competing priority.
