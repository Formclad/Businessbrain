Version 6.1 | 23 September 2026
Owner: Sofia (General Manager)

The method for every job from here on. Not a fix for existing jobs.
Supersedes v5.0 and all earlier Extent of Works / Project Setup notes.

v6.0 is written from Sofia's tested sequence of 21 September. Three things
changed from v5.0 and they are marked CHANGED where they appear. v6.1 adds

=====================================================================
THE STRUCTURE, IN SIX LINES
=====================================================================

  Assembly    = the job, building or stage. Usually one per job.
  Fixtures    = the work packages. One level. Never cost types.
  Materials   = sit inside their work-package fixture.
  Labour      = ONE "Installation Labour" line inside each fixture.
  Hours tab   = EMPTY at tender. Always.
  Breakdown   = the claim spine, imported from the Group IDs.

Standard work packages:
  Roof Covering · Roof Plumbing · Roof Ancillaries · Cladding · Cladding
  Ancillaries

A roof-only job runs three of them. Hire, freight and subcontractors are
Items, not fixtures.


=====================================================================
THE ONE THING PEOPLE WILL GET WRONG
=====================================================================

"Installation Labour" is a LINE INSIDE a fixture.
It is NEVER a fixture of its own.

This is one word apart from the mistake that broke PAE. There, Installation
Labour and Delivery & Handling were built as fixtures under every assembly,
and activating extents produced twelve rows — four of them called
Installation Labour — which is what the crew would have had to choose from.

Right:   Roof Plumbing (fixture)
           └ materials
           └ Installation Labour  ← a line
Wrong:   Roof Plumbing (fixture)
         Installation Labour (fixture)   ← this is the disease

If in doubt: fixtures are named after WORK. Lines are named after COST.

=====================================================================
PART A – TENDER  (Mairead)
=====================================================================

A1. Efficaxone take-off arrives. Reconcile into the import workbook as now.

A2. TICK "ENABLE BREAKDOWNS?" – Tender > Modify, right column, below
    Assistant Estimator. Do this FIRST, before building the estimate,
    because it adds the Group IDs column to the grids you are about to
    fill in.

    (The Nexvia manual calls this "Commercial Tender". The screen does not.)

    SET DEFAULT WASTE TO 0% – Estimate tab, top of screen, beside Default   NEW
    Margin. Wastage is allowed once, in the take-off and workbook, following
    Estimating Rules of Thumb. Efficaxone quantities usually include it
    already; Nexvia's default would add it again.

    Verify: open one imported material line and confirm it carries no waste.

A3. BUILD THE ASSEMBLY. One assembly named for the job or building.
    Fixtures are the work packages. Under each fixture:

      - the materials for that package
      - ONE line named "Installation Labour"

    Assign each fixture its Breakdown Group ID as you go — B-2 Sheeting,
    B-3 Plumbing, and so on. Group IDs do not inherit from a parent row.

A4. LEAVE THE HOURS TAB COMPLETELY EMPTY.                        CHANGED
    Hours entered there are budgeted a second time on top of the labour
    lines inside the fixtures. The project's Budget > Hours will still
    show figures once converted — those arrive from the fixture lines and
    are correct. It is only the TENDER Hours tab that stays empty.

    Verify: Estimate > Summary. Hours and costs match the quoted scope,
    with no duplicated labour.

A5. ITEMS. Hire, crane, EWP, scaffold, freight, subcontractors. These do
    not import from the assembly file and are entered by hand. Give each
    one a Group ID too, or it lands in Unassigned Costs at A6. Keep the
    same item names on every job so bills code consistently.

A6. IMPORT THE BREAKDOWN.                                        CHANGED
    Estimate > BREAKDOWN > Import Breakdown. It builds itself from the
    Group IDs assigned at A3 and A5. There is no need to create the
    headings by hand and tag them afterwards, which is what v5.0 said.

    Verify: total sales value equals the quoted contract amount, and
    nothing is sitting in an unassigned row. If the two differ, something
    was never given a Group ID.

    Do NOT press "Apply to All" next to Default Margin. It overwrites every
    row's margin and wipes the real figures. Set margins where the cost
    lives.

A7. Commercial review. Whole-job margin against Pricing Settings —
    30–32% target, 28% review floor. The tender Default Margin is 25%,     NEW
    which is below the floor: do not leave lines on the default.

A8. Export the Pricing Full Breakdown report into the tender folder,       NEW
    dated, and run the Estimate Review Agent before submission.


=====================================================================
PART B – JOB WON  (target: 10 minutes)
=====================================================================

B1. Convert the tender to a project.

B2. Summary > Modify. Confirm:
       Enable Cost to Complete             TICKED
       Cost to Complete – Use Extents?     NOT TICKED
       Mobile Time Tracking                TICKED
       Production Time Tracking            TICKED
    Save.

    Verify: Budget > Cost to Complete shows your trade Breakdown scopes,
    not a list of individual extents.

B3. Budget > Hours. Confirm the hours came across from the fixture labour
    lines and the rate reads $80.00. This is the last easy moment to
    correct the rate.

B4. ACTIVATE EXTENTS, THEN STRIP THEM BACK.                      CHANGED
    Budget > ASSEMBLY > Options > Activate Extent of Work.

    Select and DELETE every row that is not an Installation Labour line,
    so only the clean labour lines remain. v5.0 said to leave the rows as
    they are; that was wrong and produces extents the crew has to wade
    through.

    Do NOT press Create Group or Create All. Those strip out the work
    packages and replace them with labour-type lumps.

    Then Create Extent of Works.

    Verify: Project Tools > Extent of Works shows one extent per work
    package on this job — Sheeting, Plumbing, Penetrations and so on —
    each carrying its quoted hours.

    This runs ONCE per project. Later edits go through Admin portal >
    project > Extent of Works.

B5. Budget > Items. Confirm the hire and freight rows are there.

B6. Open the app on the job. The crew should see one entry per work
    package.

B7. Set the project status. It should not read "Pending" once work starts.


=====================================================================
PART C – RUNNING THE JOB
=====================================================================

C1. Installers log daily hours in the Nexvia mobile app by selecting the
    specific Extent — "Roof Plumbing – Installation Labour". The labour
    type follows from the extent. Nothing needs assigning by hand.

    Verify: Budget > Cost to Complete. Actual cost burns down the matching
    Breakdown row, and Not Allocated reads $0.00.

C2. Raise purchase orders against the assembly or item group. Bills then
    match the PO line and inherit the coding instead of arriving uncoded.

C3. Bills without a PO get coded on receipt, not in a monthly batch.

C4. Variations entered when they happen, assigned to a breakdown heading.


=====================================================================
PART D – CLAIMING
=====================================================================

D1. Budget > Summary > BUDGET CHECKED first, every time. A claim raised
    against a stale budget carries a red alert and will not reconcile.

D2. Accounts > Client Progress Claims > Import Claim Data.
    Mode: PROJECT BREAKDOWN, then choose the breakdown imported at A6.

    Why not the others:
      Project Budget              one "Eow Total" line for the whole job
      Assembly Only               fixtures only — hire items never appear
      Project Budget + Assemblies items appear, but so does a separate
                                  Hours total alongside the fixture labour.
                                  On PAE both read $2,760.12 — claim both
                                  and labour is billed twice.

D3. Check the preview, tick the confirmation, Update claim data.

D4. Percentage complete per line. Variations claim separately.

D5. LOCK TOTALS.

D6. Never change the client on Summary > Modify after claiming. It wipes
    the entire Client Progress Claim section.


=====================================================================
WHAT IS PROVEN AND WHAT IS NOT
=====================================================================

Proven on the live system:

  Fixture = Extent of Work, one to one, top level only. Nested fixtures
  never become extents.

  No extent means no labour type. Tested on PAE: a project with no extents
  offers the crew no labour type dropdown at all, and every hour lands in
  Unassigned. This is why Our Lady has 157.5 hours unallocated.

  Cost-type fixtures break both. PAE's V4 structure produced twelve
  extents, four called Installation Labour.

  Assembly Costs exclude fixture labour, so Cost to Complete does not
  double count.

  The breakdown reports margin, it does not add it. On Weeks Group HQ,
  B-4 Roofing Hire Items shows $12,751.42 — exactly the sum of its item
  figures. The blended percentage is a weighted average.

  Group IDs tag at fixture level. Seven tags covered the whole of Weeks
  Group HQ.

Not yet proven — check on the first job run this way:

  Extents existing while "Use Extents" is OFF. The reasoning is that
  extents give the crew something to book against, which assigns the
  labour type, while the CTC setting decides whether COST reporting runs
  through extents instead of assemblies. C1's verify step is the test:
  book one hour, confirm it lands against its labour type, and confirm
  Not Allocated reads $0.00.

  If it does not work, the fallback is to leave Use Extents ON and set
  exclusions to hide assembly costs. Since fixtures are the work packages,
  that still reports cost per work package by a different route.

  Whether a tender's Default Waste setting applies to lines that are
  imported, or only to lines added by hand. A2's verify step is the test.


=====================================================================
ONE DECISION STILL OPEN
=====================================================================

The work packages have two sets of names in circulation. Friday's standard
was Roof Covering, Roof Plumbing, Roof Ancillaries, Cladding, Cladding
Ancillaries. The tested sequence used Sheeting, Plumbing and Penetrations,
and live tenders built on V7.3 (e.g. Estia Salisbury) use Roof Covering &
Insulation, Roof Ancillaries and Roof Plumbing.

They describe the same work. Pick one set and use it on every job, because
consistent naming is what lets bills code themselves and lets one job's
figures be compared with another's. Until it is settled, this document
keeps Friday's five.


=====================================================================
WHAT THIS GIVES YOU
=====================================================================

  Cost per work package        materials and labour under the same names
  Labour with no hand-sorting  the crew's extent selection does it
  Bills coded on arrival       consistent assemblies and item groups
  Claims that reconcile        same headings on the budget and the claim
  Estimating feedback          actual hours against Labour_Factors
  A job that looks like every other job, which is what makes any of it
  automatable

A light version is defensible on small jobs — extents only, no breakdown,
simple claim. Write the threshold down rather than deciding job by job.
