FORMCLAD – PROJECT DELIVERY, CONTRACT TO CLOSE-OUT – CURRENT
Version 1.1 | 23 September 2026
Owner: Sofia (General Manager) for the commercial side; Travis (Director) for the site side.

WHY THIS EXISTS
The Brain already covers the commercial ends of a job: contract review
(Subcontract Agreements), Nexvia setup (Nexvia Setup Procedure), variations,
claims and close-out. Nothing covered the middle — handover, measuring,
ordering, bookings, scheduling, and who talks to the builder about what.
This document is that middle, and the spine that links the others in order.
Where another document holds the detail, this one points to it rather than
repeating it.


=====================================================================
WHO DOES WHAT
=====================================================================

Formclad has no full-time project manager. The role is split BY TYPE OF
WORK, not by job, matching the Approval Authorities:

  SOFIA – COMMERCIAL PM. Contract, Nexvia setup, purchase orders, pricing
    and submitting variations, claims, retention, job result.
    Works Formclad Mondays and Fridays (Thursday pay only).

  TRAVIS – SITE PM. Scope, programme, measuring sign-off, order quantities,
    crew allocation and the labour board, quality, identifying and agreeing
    variations on site. Moves between jobs.

  SITE SUPERVISOR (leading hand on each job). Travis's eyes and hands on
    site when he is elsewhere. See DELEGATION below.

  MAIREAD – handover pack from the estimate.
  MARIANNE – POs, bills, claim issue and receipt tracking.

THE RULE THAT MAKES THE SPLIT WORK: anything that cannot wait until Monday
or Friday sits with Travis or the site supervisor, never with Sofia.


=====================================================================
BUILDER CONTACT – TWO CHANNELS, ONE INBOX
=====================================================================

Builders run two channels themselves: a contracts administrator or project
manager in the office, and a site manager or supervisor on site. Formclad
mirrors that. Each channel has one Formclad owner, so the builder is never
answered by two people saying different things.

  OFFICE CHANNEL – Sofia.
    Contract, programme DOCUMENT, drawings and revisions, prequalification,
    insurances, portal access, variations (pricing and submission), claims,
    payment, retention.

  SITE CHANNEL – Travis (site supervisor day to day).
    Start dates, access, set-out, deliveries, crane days, hold points,
    daily coordination, defects walk.

  WHO SENDS THE PROGRAMME REQUEST. Whoever already holds the contract
  thread — normally Sofia, because the programme is part of contract
  review: you cannot check programme dates, LDs or EOT exposure without it.
  Ask in the same reply as the contract acknowledgement, cc Travis.
  Once work is near, Travis takes over the LIVE programme with the site
  manager (look-aheads, date changes). The document comes via Sofia; the
  day-to-day dates come via Travis.

  CC RULE. Every builder email on a job cc's the other PM and
  admin@formclad.com.au, so nothing lives only in one person's inbox and
  nothing waits for Sofia's Monday. Anything with a clock on it (variation
  notice, EOT notice, defect notice) is answered by whoever sees it first.

  Record the builder's two contacts in the contract summary and the
  Builder Profile.


=====================================================================
DELEGATION – WHAT THE SITE SUPERVISOR CAN DO
=====================================================================

Principle: the supervisor DOES and RECORDS; Travis DECIDES and COMMITS.
Anything that changes money, scope or dates with the builder stays with
Travis.

  SUPERVISOR CAN DO                         TRAVIS KEEPS
  Check-measure and record it               Signs off the order list
  Daily hours check in the Nexvia app       Weekly % complete per package
  Pre-start / toolbox, SWMS sign-on         Writes / approves the SWMS
  Check deliveries against the order        Decides replacement / back order
  Photos, site diary, ITP and install       Final quality sign-off
    checks in the app
  Spot a variation: photo it, note who      Agrees the variation with the
    directed it, tell Travis SAME DAY         builder; confirms in writing
  Day-to-day coordination with the          Commits to programme dates,
    builder's site supervisor                 access or sequence changes
  Report shortages, damage, delays          Escalation triggers (Part A)

Supervisor must NEVER: agree to extra work or a price, accept a changed
start date, or sign builder paperwork that changes scope.

The supervisor needs: Nexvia app access with checklists and audits, the
job's drawings (current revision), the order list, the SWMS, the builder
site contact's number, and the HOURS BUDGET PER PACKAGE. Crews who know
the target usually hit it.


=====================================================================
THE SEQUENCE
=====================================================================

1. CONTRACT ARRIVES (day 0)
   [ ] Receiver saves it to 01 - Commercial, "UNSIGNED [date]". (S or T)
   [ ] Reply to acknowledge. In the same email ask for: the programme,
       current drawings (issued-for-construction revision), builder site
       contact, induction and portal requirements. (Sofia, cc Travis)
   [ ] Review against the Subcontract Agreements checklist. (Sofia)
   [ ] Check programme dates and access against what was priced. (Travis)
   [ ] Anything not matching the quote qualifications: raise in writing
       BEFORE signing. (Sofia)
   [ ] Sign, save SIGNED copy, fill the one-page contract summary, diarise
       claim date and notice periods. (Sofia)
   [ ] PENCIL the job onto the labour board from the builder's programme
       and the crew-days calculation (see SCHEDULING). (Travis)

2. JOB SETUP (Sofia's next Monday)
   [ ] Convert tender to project; Nexvia Setup Procedure B1–B7.
   [ ] Contract sum, terms, retention into Nexvia; upload agreement.
   [ ] Project folder with 05 - Close-Out.
   [ ] Builder Profile checked: claim method and cut-off.
   [ ] Prequal documents sent if asked (insurances, licence, SWMS).

3. HANDOVER MEETING (30 min – Travis, Sofia, Mairead; supervisor if known)
   [ ] Walk the estimate package by package: hours, materials, wastage,
       crane/EWP/scaffold allowances, exclusions, long-lead items.
   [ ] Convert hours per package into crew-days (see SCHEDULING).
   [ ] Name the site supervisor for the job.
   OUTPUT: Travis and the supervisor know the hours budget and the
   crew-days per package. Nothing is ordered before this meeting
   (Part A: 100% handover).

4. BEFORE SITE (2–6 weeks out)
   [ ] Compare IFC drawings with the tendered revision. Differences are
       possible variations — flag now. (Travis)
   [ ] Colour, profile and product confirmed with the builder in
       writing. (Travis, via site channel or Sofia if contractual)
   [ ] Shop drawings / flashing profiles submitted and approved if
       required. (Travis)
   [ ] ORDER IN TWO STAGES:
       Stage 1 – LONG-LEAD items ordered early from drawings (special
         colours, profiles, insulation, anything weeks out).
       Stage 2 – CUT-TO-LENGTH sheets and flashings ordered only after
         the check-measure.
   [ ] Check-measure on site once purlins are up. (Supervisor or Travis;
       Travis signs off the order list)
   [ ] Large quantities quoted, not price-booked. (Sofia)
   [ ] POs raised at budgeted amount, coded to the work package. (Sofia /
       Marianne) Order placed with a delivery date.
   [ ] Crane, EWP, scaffold, edge protection booked; crane paperwork into
       the builder's portal. (Travis)
   [ ] SWMS on Master Builders SA; crew inductions booked. (Travis)
   [ ] Start date agreed with the builder's site manager; crew allocated.
       (Travis)
   [ ] PRE-START MEETING on site with the builder's site manager: access,
       set-out, deliveries, laydown, crane days, hold points. (Travis +
       supervisor)
   [ ] FIRM the job on the labour board. (Travis)

5. ON SITE – see SCHEDULING AND WEEKLY CONTROL below.

6. MONTHLY CLAIM (see Getting Paid)
   By the 18th – variations priced and approved.
   By the 20th – Travis gives % complete per package (the same figures
     as the Friday check).
   By the 23rd – Sofia: Budget Checked, Import Claim Data (Project
     Breakdown).
   By the 25th – issued, or earlier if the builder's cut-off is earlier.
   Marianne tracks payment.

7. CLOSE-OUT – start two weeks before finishing (see Job Close-Out).


=====================================================================
SCHEDULING AND WEEKLY CONTROL
=====================================================================

WHAT WE DO AND DO NOT SCHEDULE
With five field staff, the question that matters each week is WHO IS
WHERE, not what hour a single task starts. So:
  YES – one labour board across all jobs.
  YES – crew-days per work package, worked out from the quoted hours.
  NO  – a detailed per-job Gantt chart in Nexvia or anywhere else. It
        takes hours to build and is out of date by the first rain day.
        The builder's programme and two-week look-ahead do that job.
Revisit this if the field team grows well beyond five or jobs start
overlapping heavily.

CREW-DAYS FROM QUOTED HOURS
  Quoted hours for the package ÷ (crew size × about 7 productive hours)
  = days on site.
  Example: 168 hours of Roof Covering with a crew of 3 → about 8 days.
  Add crane days, builder hold points and access set-up. That is the
  job's duration and it goes on the labour board.
  Use productive hours, not paid hours — the same basis the pricing uses.

THE LABOUR BOARD
  Rows: the five field employees (and Travis when on tools).
  Columns: the next 4–6 weeks.
  Cells: the job each person is on. Also mark leave, crane days,
  inductions and deliveries.
  Where it lives: a whiteboard or one shared sheet in SharePoint.
    Check first whether Nexvia has a scheduling or calendar feature (ask
    Tosin). If it does, use it rather than adding another tool. Do not
    buy a separate scheduling product.
  When a job goes on:
    Pencilled – when the contract and programme arrive (step 1).
    Firmed – at the pre-start meeting (step 4).
    Reviewed – every Monday against each builder's two-week look-ahead.
  Owner: Travis. The board is what shows two jobs landing in the same
  week before it happens, not after.

THE WEEK ON A LIVE JOB
  MONDAY (Travis)
    [ ] Update the labour board from each builder's look-ahead.
    [ ] Check orders and deliveries needed for the next two weeks.
    [ ] Confirm each supervisor knows this week's packages and their
        hours target.
  DAILY (Supervisor)
    [ ] Pre-start / toolbox; SWMS sign-on.
    [ ] Crew log hours in the Nexvia app against the right work package
        before the cut-off; supervisor checks nothing is unassigned.
    [ ] Deliveries checked on arrival; shortages and damage reported.
    [ ] Photos, diary, ITP and installation checks in the app.
    [ ] Variations flagged to Travis the same day (see Variations).
  FRIDAY (Travis, then Sofia)
    [ ] THE HOURS CHECK, per work package:
          % complete        – how far through is it?
          hours used        – from the app
          hours remaining   – Travis's honest estimate to finish
        If hours used is well ahead of % complete (for example 60% of
        hours gone at 40% done), that is the warning. Over 10% forecast
        overrun is a Part A escalation trigger.
    [ ] Sofia pulls the reports and asks Claude for the weekly review
        (see Weekly Routine). Anything site-related goes back to Travis
        as a specific question.
  MONTHLY
    The Friday % complete figures ARE the claim percentages. One set of
    numbers, not two.

WHY THIS MATTERS
An overrun caught in week two can still be fixed — more crew, a
different sequence, a variation claim, a conversation with the builder.
Caught at claim time, it is just a loss.


=====================================================================
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
=====================================================================
- The builder always knows who to call about what, and gets one answer.
- Nothing time-critical waits for Sofia's next Formclad day.
- Every field employee's next four weeks are visible on one board.
- Every live package has % complete, hours used and hours remaining
  updated each Friday.
- No sheet is ordered off drawings alone.
- No variation is built on a verbal instruction.
- Any of us can open the project folder and see where the job is.
